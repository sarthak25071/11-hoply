# Phase 1 — Product Definition
*Day 1*

**You are a product team.** Working in your pair, you're going to invent a web application and define it well enough that a competent development team could build it.

You pick the product. Anything goes, as long as it's:

- a **web app** in the same recommended stack as the group project (runs in a browser),
- **realistic to build** — two developers should be able to build a meaningful version of it in about 1.5 day,
- **self-contained** — no paid services, special hardware, or data you don't actually have.

Stuck for an idea? A sheep breeding tracker, a health tracker, a small event RSVP tool, a puppy adoption center, a flashcard trainer, a "what should we watch tonight" group vote.

---

## What you'll hand over

By the end of the afternoon, you have created complete **product brief**. Include:

**1. Vision — one paragraph.**
What is this, who is it for, and why would they use it instead of doing nothing? Somewhere in it, finish this sentence: *"This is a success if a user can ______."*

**2. Users.**
Two or three user personas, a couple of lines each. Who are they, what do they want, how tech savvy are they? Etc

**3. User stories with acceptance criteria on Github Project Board.**
Use this format, and write 10–15 of them, **ordered by priority**:
> As a **[user]**, I want **[capability]**, so that **[benefit]**.
> **Done when:** three to five concrete, checkable conditions.

**4. Scope.**
Split your stories into **Must-have (MVP)** and **Nice-to-have**. Then list your **non-goals** — things you are deliberately *not* building. 

**5. Key screens / mockups.**
Low-fidelity is fine: paper, a whiteboard photo, boxes and arrows, Figma — whatever. Show the main screens and how a user moves between them.

**6. Data & rules.**
What are the core "things" in the app (a task, a recipe, a vote…) and their important fields? Any rules that aren't obvious? ("A user can only vote once." "A title is required.")


---

## About the implementation

Describe **what** the product does and **why** — not **how** to build it.

---

## Checkpoint — the 3-minute pitch

Right before the afternoon ends, each pair pitches their product to the class room.

- **3 minutes. Hard stop.** A timer runs. At 3:00 you're cut off mid-sentence — plan for it.
- Cover: what it is, who it's for, and the top two or three things it *must* do. Skip the backstory.
- This is deliberate practice at being concise under a real constraint. 
- Don't use slides, show us your output.

**Save your brief in a github repo with a project board connected to it. Hand your finished brief to your instructor at the end of the day, it should be a public github link.**
